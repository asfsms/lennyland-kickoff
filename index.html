For grown-ups: sources and accuracy

The parade took place on the night of May 17, 1884, about a year after the bridge opened on May 24, 1883. Contemporary reports list 21 elephants, 7 camels and 10 dromedaries, which later accounts shorten to 17 camels. Most accounts put Jumbo at the rear of the line; a few say he led it. Jumbo's weight is usually given as 6 to 6.5 tons.

The story leaves out why New Yorkers were nervous: a week after the opening, a rumor that the bridge was collapsing caused a crowd panic on the stairs and 12 people died. Barnum had offered the elephant walk before the opening and was turned down; the 1884 walk was also publicity for his circus.

Numbers used: construction 1869 to 1883 (14 years); tower tops 276.5 feet above high water and 135 feet of clearance at mid-span (the shot uses 276 and 135); four main cables, about 15.75 inches thick, each with more than 5,000 wires (5,434 is the usual count, and sources vary). The cables were designed to be about six times stronger than needed, later judged closer to four times because a supplier slipped in poor wire. The cable toy shows the idea, not real strength figures.

Sources: History.com on the elephant walk; Brooklyn Waterfront History; American Society of Civil Engineers; Wikipedia on Jumbo; National Trust on Emily Roebling.
